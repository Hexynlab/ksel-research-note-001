# KSEL Research Notes
## Evidence Governance for Intelligent Systems

**H3XYN LAB — Technical Research Notes**  
**Series:** KSEL · **Repository status:** Active research publication  
**Last updated:** 22 November 2026

KSEL (Knowledge, Source, Evidence Layer) is a proposed governance architecture for making evidence traceable, uncertainty inspectable, and review decisions auditable in intelligent systems.

This repository publishes the KSEL technical research-note series. Each note develops a defined part of the research programme. Publications distinguish proposed architecture and hypotheses from experimentally validated findings.

## Publications

### KSEL Research Note 001
**Evidence Governance, Uncertainty Management, and Review Controls for Intelligent Systems**

Establishes the conceptual foundation: evidence provenance, claim-to-evidence relationships, multidimensional uncertainty, contradiction handling, review states, auditability, and a proposed evaluation programme.

[Read Research Note 001](research/KSEL-RESEARCH-NOTE-001.md)

### KSEL Research Note 002
**Cost-Aware Evidence Selection and Adaptive Review Governance**

Develops decision-centred evidence acquisition, candidate evidence-action records, cost-aware Value of Information, adaptive review tiers, evidence sufficiency, stopping conditions, and reassessment governance.

[Read Research Note 002](research/KSEL-RESEARCH-NOTE-002.md)

## Research position

Both publications are preliminary research frameworks. Their hypotheses and evaluation measures are proposed for future testing; they are not presented as validated findings, certified standards, or proof of system performance.

The series distinguishes source evidence, claims, inference, and recommendations. It treats mandatory safety, privacy, security, and review requirements as constraints rather than costs that can simply be traded away.

## Research integrity

H3XYN LAB aims to make research claims proportionate to available evidence. Future implementation and evaluation work should document methods, assumptions, baselines, limitations, and reproducibility information. No experimental result should be inferred from the existence of a proposed framework.
